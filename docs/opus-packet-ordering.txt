Opus receive ordering: diagnosis and correction (2026-11-03)
==========================================================

Problem
-------
Speech from every NVDA voice and music were distorted over a remote Tailscale
connection, while the same setup previously sounded normal on the local network.
Lower volume, bypassed speech processing, and a larger iOS maximum delay did not
resolve it. Diagnostics showed about 400 packets/second, nearly equal initially
missing and late-arrival counts, and frequent buffer trims. These counters include
packets that later arrive; they do not demonstrate 25% permanent packet loss.
The observations do not establish Tailscale as the cause of packet reordering.

The original decoder processes arrival order. With 2.5 ms CELT packets arriving as
100, 102, 101, 103, it inserts replacement audio for 101 before decoding 102, then
decodes the late 101 and rewinds its expected sequence. It subsequently inserts
replacement audio for 102 again. Four packets produce six audio slots (15 ms
instead of 10 ms). A post-decode jitter buffer cannot repair codec state or restore
the original order, and must repeatedly trim the extra audio.

Correction
----------
StreamSession decrypts each packet and orders compressed payloads before feeding
the stateful Opus decoder. In-order packets pass through immediately. A gap can
wait for 60 ms; a maintenance timer checks every 20 ms (plus scheduling leeway),
including when no subsequent packet arrives. This is a recovery window, not an
unconditional added delay for every packet.

Sequence comparison handles UInt32 wraparound. Played/concealed and duplicate
packets cannot rewind the decoder. The queue is bounded at 512 packets; a larger
forward jump resets the queue and decoder. Expired gaps get at most 100 ms of
concealment, with a decoder reset for larger gaps. CELT uses PLC because it carries
no in-band FEC. SILK/hybrid can use the next packet's FEC for only the immediately
preceding missing slot. Receive and timer work share one decoder lock; the engine
releases its own lock before flushing sessions.

Diagnostics distinguish initial arrival gaps, queued packets emitted in order,
deadline misses, ignored stale/duplicate packets, replacement slots, decoder
resynchronizations, and decode errors. Copy all details includes the minute's
diagnostics, previously visible only in the UI.

Verification
------------
Tests exercise ordering, duplicates, sequence wraparound, terminal-gap draining,
multiple missing packets, large jumps, and a full minute of synthetic one-in-four
reordering. Pipeline tests use real AES-GCM and libopus, require non-silent output,
and compare reordered playback sample-for-sample with in-order playback. They also
check one-time CELT concealment, discarded late arrivals, and bounded long gaps.

The upstream PR version passes all 276 Swift tests and unsigned Release builds for
both iOS and macOS using Xcode 27.1 on the existing Mac build host.

A separate iOS TestFlight diagnostic build, based on v1.0.0 with this receive fix
and an original-decoder comparison switch, passed 273 tests. The affected user
confirmed normal audio on the previously distorted remote connection after
installing that build. The Windows sender and remote-access services were unchanged.
The upstream change keeps ordering always enabled and omits the test app's branding,
Apple account/build settings, manual-start screen, and comparison-only legacy path.

This fixes reorder-induced decoder corruption and extra audio. It cannot recover
arbitrarily late/lost packets, eliminate WAN latency, or guarantee uninterrupted
playback through a long network outage. The 60 ms window is fixed in this version.
